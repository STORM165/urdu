Core Fundamentals

How Urdu Cursive Script Works

DEVELOPED BY FARHAN

Urdu is written from Right to Left. Most letters connect together by dropping their big tails and becoming small hooks, EXCEPT for 7 Non-Connector Letters that break the connection line!

1

The 7 Non-Connector "Naughty" Letters

Remember these by heart! They NEVER connect to any letter coming AFTER them.

Rule: A non-connector letter WILL join to a letter coming before it, but it creates a gap and forces the next letter to start in its isolated form.

2

Letter Families & Short Joining Hooks

When letters connect at the start or middle of a word, they drop their lower loops and become short hooks.

Be-Family (ب, پ, ت, ٹ, ث)

Boat shape
Isolated
ب
Initial Hook
بـ
Example "Baat"
بات

Rule: Cut off the flat boat body. Keep only a single vertical tooth/hook and position the dots.

Jeem-Family (ج, چ, ح, خ)

Belly shape
Isolated
ج
Chop the Belly
جـ
Example "Jaan"
جان

Rule: Drop the big lower loop completely. Keep only the top triangular head and the dots.

Seen/Sheen (س, ش)

Teeth shape
Isolated
س
Initial Teeth
سـ
Example "Sach"
سچ

Rule: Keep the 3 small teeth at the front, drop the big round tail.

Kaf/Gaf (ک, گ)

Slanted stick
Isolated
ک
Initial Hook
کـ
Example "Kaam"
کام

Rule: Keep the upper slanted stick and loop directly into the next letter.
